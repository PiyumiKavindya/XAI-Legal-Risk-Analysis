| Paper Title | Explainable AI under Contract and Tort Law: Legal Incentives and Technical Challenges |
| Authors | Philipp Hacker, Ralf Krestel, Stefan Grundmann, Felix Naumann |
| Year | 2020 |
| Research Domain | Explainable AI (XAI), Legal AI, Contract Law, Tort Law |
| Research Problem | Black-box AI makes it difficult for professionals to justify AI-assisted decisions, creating legal liability concerns under contract and tort law. |
| Research Objective | To investigate how explainable AI affects legal liability and whether professionals should use explainable AI to meet legal standards of care. |
| Proposed Solution | A legal-technical framework that balances explainability and prediction accuracy using two legal case studies (medical diagnosis and corporate M&A) and an ML experiment. |
| Methodology | Legal analysis + Case studies + Machine Learning experiment (SMS spam classification). |
| Dataset | SMS Spam Collection Dataset (5,574 SMS messages). Used only to demonstrate the explainability vs. accuracy trade-off. |
| Models Compared | Naïve Bayes, Decision Tree, SVM, kNN, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, CNN. |
| Key Findings | Explainability is important not only for GDPR but also for contract law and tort law. AI systems should balance explainability and accuracy while maintaining human oversight. |
| Strengths | Interdisciplinary research, practical legal case studies, explains legal importance of XAI, connects AI and law. |
| Limitations | No legal contract analysis system, no legal dataset, no temporal compliance, no LLMs, no clause-level risk assessment, no real-world implementation. |
| Explicit Research Gap | Existing research mainly focuses on GDPR; explainability under contract law and tort law has been largely overlooked. |
| Implicit Research Gap | No explainable legal contract analysis, no temporal compliance monitoring, no contract risk assessment, no LLM integration, no end-to-end legal AI system. |
| Novelty | Introduces a legal perspective on explainable AI by linking explainability with contract law, tort law, and professional liability rather than only GDPR. |
| Future Work | Improve interpretable AI methods, develop better explanation techniques, validate in real-world domains, encourage interdisciplinary collaboration. |
| Key Contributions | Extends explainability beyond GDPR, proposes legal guidelines for AI adoption, analyzes the explainability–accuracy trade-off, supports arguments with legal case studies and ML experiments. |
| Relevance to My Research | Provides the legal foundation for explainable AI but does not implement contract intelligence, temporal compliance monitoring, or explainable contract risk assessment, leaving opportunities for my proposed research. |
